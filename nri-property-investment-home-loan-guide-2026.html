1. RBI & FEMA Rules — NRI Property Investment

The legal framework governing real estate acquisitions by non-residents in India is codified under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999, read alongside the Foreign Exchange Management (Acquisition and Transfer of Immovable Property in India) Regulations, 2018 issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). For global investors holding Non-Resident Indian (NRI), Overseas Citizen of India (OCI), or Person of Indian Origin (PIO) status, the Indian real estate market offers unrestricted entry into residential and commercial sectors, provided statutory banking channels are maintained.

Key Regulatory Takeaways under FEMA 1999:

  • General Permission for Residential & Commercial Real Estate: NRIs and OCIs do not require prior permission from the RBI to purchase or inherit residential apartments, villas, builder floors, or commercial office spaces and retail shops in India.
  • Permissible Payment Channels: All financial considerations for property purchase must be routed strictly through legitimate banking channels:
    • Direct inward remittance from abroad in convertible foreign currency via SWIFT / TT.
    • Funds debited from Non-Resident External (NRE) bank account.
    • Funds debited from Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR-B) account.
    • Funds debited from Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) bank account.
  • Strict Prohibition on Agricultural Lands: NRIs, OCIs, and PIOs are strictly prohibited from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses anywhere in India. This restriction applies regardless of location or state land laws. However, an NRI may inherit agricultural property from a resident Indian.
  • Ban on Cash Transactions & Travelers Cheques: Consideration paid via cash, travelers cheques, or foreign currency notes physically brought to India is illegal and constitutes a severe violation under Section 13 of FEMA.
  • Banker Notification & Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC): Investors must inform their Authorized Dealer Category-I (AD Cat-I) bank about the property remittance and secure a valid FIRC / BRC (Bank Realisation Certificate) for subsequent tax and repatriation compliance.

Important Legal Notice: Maintaining complete audit trails of all money transfers, builder receipts, allotment letters, and banking statements is vital. When repatriating sale proceeds or rental income in future years, Indian tax authorities and AD banks require historic FIRCs proving that the original acquisition was funded out of foreign convertible exchange or eligible NRE/FCNR balances.

2. Power of Attorney (PoA) — Execution Do's & Don'ts

Because NRIs reside overseas, executing physical property documents, visiting sub-registrar offices, inspecting construction sites, and signing home loan agreements often requires appointing a trusted representative in India via a Power of Attorney (PoA). However, improper execution or overly broad powers can lead to legal disputes, unauthorized transfers, or rejection by sub-registrars and financial institutions.

✅ DO'S for NRI Power of Attorney

  • Use a Specific Power of Attorney (SPA): Draft an SPA restricted specifically to the target property. Clearly mention the survey number, plot/flat number, boundary details, building name, and city.
  • Notarization & Consular Attestation / Apostille: The PoA must be executed in the country of residence. If the country is a signatory to the Hague Convention (e.g., USA, UK, UAE, Australia), get it Apostilled. Otherwise, get it notarized and attested by the local Indian Embassy / Consulate.
  • Adjudication in India: Within 3 months of receiving the original PoA in India, the attorney holder must present it to the District Collector / Sub-Registrar of Assurances for adjudication and payment of stamp duty as per state laws.
  • Define Scope & Validity Period: Explicitly state the exact powers granted (e.g., signing agreement to sell, representing before RERA, obtaining electricity/water connections, negotiating home loan terms). Specify a calendar expiry date or condition for revocation.
  • Prefer Immediate Relatives: Execute the PoA in favor of trusted close relatives (parents, spouse, siblings, adult children) to avoid stamp duty surcharges levied by certain states on non-blood relatives.

❌ DON'TS for NRI Power of Attorney

  • Avoid General Power of Attorney (GPA): Do not issue broad GPAs giving sweeping rights to buy, sell, mortgage, or transfer any property. Following Supreme Court rulings (e.g., Suraj Lamp & Industries case), GPA sale transfers are legally void in India.
  • Do NOT Grant Unlimited Financial Powers: Never give the attorney holder authority to collect sale proceeds in their personal bank account. All financial transactions must flow exclusively through the NRI's NRE/NRO account.
  • Do NOT Execute Undated or Blank PoAs: Signing undated, unnumbered, or blank PoA forms provided by developers or brokers is extremely dangerous and constitutes a major fraud risk.
  • Minors Cannot Be Appointed: NRIs cannot execute a PoA in favor of a minor child (under 18 years of age).
  • Do Not Omit Revocation Clauses: Always include a clause reserving the NRI principal's absolute right to revoke the PoA at any time by issuing written notice.

3. TDS on NRI Property Transactions (Section 195 Matrix)

One of the most complex aspects of NRI real estate transactions in India is the statutory Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) mechanism under Section 195 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Unlike resident property transactions where TDS is deducted at a flat 1% under Section 194-IA (for property value ≥ ₹50 Lakh), property sales, leases, or professional payments involving non-residents attract significantly higher withholding tax rates.

Transaction Type TDS Statutory Rate Effective Rate (Incl. Surcharge & Cess) Who Deducts? Timing of Deduction
Sale of Immovable Property (LTCG > 24 Months) 20% (or 12.5% under Finance Act 2024 rules) 20.8% to 23.92% (depending on capital gains tier) Buyer (Resident or Non-Resident) At or before payment/credit to NRI account
Sale of Immovable Property (STCG ≤ 24 Months) 30% (Applicable Tax Slab) 31.2% to 35.88% Buyer At or before payment/credit
Rent / Lease Payments to NRI Landlord 30% 31.2% (Plus Surcharge if annual rent > ₹50L) Tenant (Must obtain TAN under Sec 195) At or before monthly payment
Real Estate Commission / Brokerage Fee 30% 31.2% Payer (NRI seller / buyer) At or before payment
Professional / Legal / Architect Fees 10% 10.4% Payer At or before payment

💡 Critical Tax Tip: Lower or Nil TDS Certificate (Form 13)

By default, buyers deduct Section 195 TDS on the total sale value of the property rather than actual capital gains. This causes huge capital lock-ups. To avoid this, the NRI seller can submit Form 13 online to the Assessing Officer (AO) along with a Chartered Accountant (CA) Certificate, requesting a Lower/Nil TDS Certificate. Once approved, the buyer is legally authorized to deduct TDS only on the net taxable capital gain at a reduced rate, freeing up liquidity for immediate repatriation.

4. Home Loans for NRI — Key Points & Document Checklist

Major Indian public sector banks (e.g., SBI, Bank of Baroda, Canara Bank) and private financial institutions (e.g., HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra) offer dedicated NRI Home Loan products for purchasing ready-to-move flats, under-construction projects, plot purchase + construction, and major home renovation.

Loan Terms & Eligibility

  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Up to 75%–80% of property cost (varies by loan slab and property appraisal).
  • Tenure: Flexible repayment options up to 20 to 30 years (subject to age limit at loan maturity, usually 60–65 years).
  • Interest Rates: Linked to RBI Repo Rate (EBLR) or Marginal Cost of Funds (MCLR), offering parity with resident Indian interest rates.

Repayment Rules

  • Repayment Currency: EMI repayments must be made in Indian Rupees (INR).
  • Allowed Funding Channels: Debits strictly from NRE account, NRO account, or direct inward foreign remittance via banking channels.
  • Rental Offset: Rental income credited to the landlord's NRO account can be auto-debited for monthly EMI servicing.

Mandatory Document Checklist for NRI Home Loan Approval:

Identity & Overseas Residence
  • Valid Indian Passport copy with active Visa page / Work Permit.
  • OCI Card / PIO Card copy (for foreign passport holders).
  • Overseas Address Proof (Driving License, Utility Bills, Resident Card, Employer Letter).
  • PAN Card copy (Mandatory).
Employment & Income Proof
  • Latest 3 to 6 months overseas Salary Slips / Pay Stubs.
  • Employment Contract / Appointment Letter (attested by HR / Indian Embassy).
  • Latest 6 to 12 months Overseas Bank Account Statement (showing salary credits).
  • Latest 6 months NRE / NRO Bank Account Statements in India.
  • Overseas Tax Returns (W2 / P60 / Form 16 / Tax Assessment certificates).
Property & Legal Docs
  • Copy of Registered Sale Deed / Builder Agreement to Sell.
  • RERA Registration Certificate & Approved Building Floor Plans.
  • Encumbrance Certificate (EC) for past 13 to 30 years.
  • NOC from Builder / Housing Society.
  • Notarized & Adjudicated Specific Power of Attorney (if loan is executed via attorney).

5. Repatriation Matrix — What Can Be Sent Abroad?

Transferring money back to your foreign bank account (outward remittance) after selling property or receiving rental yield in India is governed by RBI's USD $1 Million Scheme per financial year (April 1 to March 31). Below is the comprehensive statutory Repatriation Matrix outlining what income streams can be remitted abroad:

Type of Remittance Stream Repatriable? Statutory Conditions & Limits
Sale Proceeds of Property (Bought via Foreign Funds / NRE) YES ✅ Up to the original principal amount remitted in foreign currency (No restriction on number of commercial properties, max 2 residential properties for principal repatriation under classic rules, capital gains remitted under $1M scheme).
Sale Proceeds of Property (Bought via NRO / Rupee Funds) YES ✅ Repatriable up to USD $1,000,000 per financial year out of NRO account balance, subject to payment of Indian capital gains taxes & Form 15CA/15CB CA certification.
Net Rental Income from Indian Property YES ✅ Current income is freely repatriable outside India after deduction of Section 195 TDS / Income Tax filing and submission of Form 15CA/15CB.
Home Loan Principal Repayment Refund YES ✅ Principal amount refunded upon deal cancellation or pre-closure is repatriable if funded originally from NRE/FCNR accounts.
Interest on Home Loan NO ❌ Interest paid to Indian bank is an expense item, not a repatriable fund stream (though deductible against Indian rental income under Section 24b).
Gifts of Property / Sale Proceeds from Relatives YES ✅ Subject to USD $1M annual limit under NRO remittance rules; cash gifts from non-relatives exceeding ₹50,000 attract Income Tax under Section 56(2)(x).
Inherited Property Sale Proceeds YES ✅ Repatriable up to USD $1M/year upon providing proof of inheritance (Will, Probate, Succession Certificate) and tax clearance.
Agricultural Land Income / Sale Proceeds NO ❌ / Restricted Agricultural income is non-taxable in India but cannot be remitted directly without special RBI prior approval. Inherited agricultural land sale proceeds must be credited to NRO account before $1M repatriation.

7. Income Tax Benefits, Capital Gains & DTAA Relief

NRIs earning rental income or realizing capital gains from property sales in India are subject to Indian Income Tax laws. However, strategic tax planning allows investors to legally minimize tax liabilities using statutory deductions and international treaties:

1. Rental Income Tax Deductions (Section 24 & Standard Deduction)

  • Standard 30% Deduction: Under Section 24(a), a flat 30% standard deduction is automatically subtracted from Net Annual Rental Value for repairs and maintenance, regardless of actual expenses.
  • Home Loan Interest Deduction (Section 24b):
    • Self-Occupied Property: Up to ₹2,000,000 (₹2 Lakh) annual deduction on interest paid.
    • Let-Out / Rented Property: Full actual interest paid on home loan is deductible against rental income (subject to overall loss set-off cap of ₹2 Lakh against other Indian income heads per financial year).
  • Principal Repayment (Section 80C): Principal repayment up to ₹150,000 (₹1.5 Lakh) per year is eligible under Section 80C (if choosing Old Tax Regime).

2. Capital Gains Exemption Routes (Section 54 & Section 54EC)

When an NRI sells a residential property held for more than 24 months (Long Term Capital Asset), the resulting Long Term Capital Gain (LTCG) can be 100% exempted from tax using:

  • Section 54 Exemption: Reinvest the net capital gains into buying another residential house property in India within 1 year before or 2 years after the sale date (or construct within 3 years). Cap on capital gain exemption under Section 54 is ₹10 Crore.
  • Section 54EC Capital Gain Bonds: Invest up to ₹50,000,000 (₹50 Lakh) of capital gains into notified infrastructure bonds issued by NHAI, REC, PFC, or IRFC within 6 months of property sale. Lock-in period is 5 years.

3. Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) Foreign Tax Credit

Under Article 6 (Income from Immovable Property) of most international DTAA treaties signed by India (e.g., with USA, UK, UAE, Canada, Singapore, Australia), real estate income is taxed in the country where the property is physically located (India). To prevent double taxation, NRIs can claim a Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) in their home residence country for the taxes paid or TDS deducted in India, eliminating double tax exposure.

8. Key Compliance Points & Quick Tips

🛡️ Essential Compliance Rules & Penalties Avoidance:

  • Mandatory ITR Filing in India: If your taxable income in India (rental yield, capital gains, interest on NRO deposits) exceeds the basic exemption limit (₹2.5 Lakh / ₹3 Lakh), filing ITR-2 or ITR-3 is legally mandatory.
  • TDS Creditable in ITR: All Section 195 TDS deducted by property buyers or tenants is reflected in your Form 26AS and AIS (Annual Information Statement). You can claim refunds if total TDS exceeds actual tax liability.
  • Severe FEMA Section 13 Penalties: Purchasing prohibited agricultural land, accepting cash consideration, or remitting funds through unofficial hawala channels attracts heavy financial penalties up to 300% (3x) of the transaction value under FEMA rules.
  • Maintain CA & Legal Retainer: Always work with qualified Indian Chartered Accountants and RERA legal specialists before executing agreements, applying for Form 13 Lower TDS certificates, or initiating Form 15CA/15CB outward remittances.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: Can an NRI or OCI buy agricultural land or farmhouses in India?

No. Under Regulation 3 of the Foreign Exchange Management Regulations, NRIs and OCIs are strictly barred from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses in India. However, they can legally inherit agricultural land from a resident Indian ancestor.

Q2: What is the TDS rate under Section 195 on the sale of property by an NRI?

When an NRI sells an immovable property, the buyer must deduct TDS under Section 195 at 20% plus applicable surcharge and cess on the total sale value (effective rate ~20.8% to 23.92%). Submitting Form 13 to the Income Tax Department allows the NRI to obtain a Lower TDS Certificate so TDS is deducted only on actual capital gains.

Q3: How much money can an NRI repatriate abroad after selling property in India?

Under the RBI $1 Million Scheme, NRIs and OCIs can repatriate up to USD $1,000,000 per financial year out of sale proceeds held in their NRO account, provided Indian taxes and TDS have been cleared and Form 15CA/15CB certification is completed by a Chartered Accountant.

Q4: Is a General Power of Attorney (GPA) valid for NRI property purchases or home loans?

No. Sub-registrars and Indian banks strictly require a Specific Power of Attorney (SPA) that explicitly identifies the target property, survey number, boundaries, and authorized acts. Following Supreme Court rulings, GPA property transfers are invalid in India.

Invest in India with Absolute Statutory Confidence

India's real estate sector presents extraordinary wealth-creation opportunities. By strictly adhering to RBI FEMA rules, executing Specific Power of Attorney, utilizing Form 13 Lower TDS certificates, and completing Form 15CA/15CB repatriation protocols, NRIs can seamlessly build and manage property portfolios in India.
